This policy says what Chess Trainer ("we", "us", the "app") keeps on your device, what it stores in the cloud, who else sees it, and which outside services your device talks to. It describes what the app actually does.

If you never sign in

Everything stays on your device, in your browser's storage: your lesson and puzzle progress, ratings and streaks, saved mistakes and brilliancies, settings, any Chess.com or Lichess usernames you link, the games imported from them (kept in your browser's database), notes you type into the feedback box, and a cache of opponents' countries looked up for the review screen. Nothing of this is sent to us. Clearing the site's data in your browser removes it.

If you sign in with Google

  • From Google. Google Firebase Authentication gives the app a user ID, your display name, your profile photo and your email address. The email is shown only to you, in the Account sheet. The app's own records do not store it; our payments setup (Stripe's Firebase extension) keeps it with your Stripe customer record.
  • What is synced to your account (Google Cloud Firestore, under your user ID): lesson progress, daily goals and streaks, game statistics, achievements, saved mistakes and brilliancies, training state, your last lesson, your ratings, Coach settings and targets, puzzle progress, your board theme and style, the computer's level, hint preferences, how many free Coach previews you have used, and the codes of your online games. Not synced: linked usernames, imported games and game reviews stay on your device.
  • Payments. Pro is handled by Stripe. We never see your card number. Stripe tells our database whether your subscription is active, and Pro depends only on that.

What other players can see

  • Online games. Your display name, photo and rating are shown to your opponent. The moves, clocks, result and the in-game chat are stored in the game's record, which only the two players can read. While an invite is waiting for its second player, anyone signed in who has the game code can see the host's name, photo, rating and the chosen clock.
  • Quick match. While you are searching, signed-in players who are also searching can see your name, photo and time control.
  • Tournaments. A tournament's name, host and players (name and photo) are visible to every signed-in player.
  • Friends. Your friend ID is your user ID. A friend request shows the recipient your name. Your friends list is visible only to you and each friend.
  • Play nearby (optional). If you turn it on, your name and a coarse area are stored so nearby players can find you: your position rounded to about 11 km, or only the area part of the postcode you type (for example the first three digits of a US ZIP code). It is removed when you turn nearby off, sign out, or delete your account.

Video calls (optional)

A call uses your camera and microphone only after you start or accept it. The call connects your device directly to your opponent's, which means each of you can see the other's internet (IP) address. Google's public STUN servers help the two devices find each other. The connection details are stored in a call record that only the two players can read. Calls are not recorded.

Photo to board (optional)

If you photograph a board, the photo is sent to our server function and to Google's Gemini service to read the position. The photo is not stored; our server's log keeps the position that was read.

Outside services your device contacts

  • Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com, fonts.gstatic.com) and www.gstatic.com (the Firebase code), on every load. Like any web request these see your IP address.
  • Google Firebase (sign-in, database, server functions), when you sign in or use online features.
  • Stripe, when you open checkout or manage your subscription.
  • Chess.com and Lichess (api.chess.com, lichess.org), only when you import games, look up opponents' countries in a review, or load Lichess puzzles. The usernames you enter are sent to them.
  • YouTube (youtube-nocookie.com), only when you open a lesson video.
  • Google STUN servers and your opponent's device, during a video call; Google Gemini, for photo to board.
  • Our website host (GitHub Pages), which serves the app.

The chess engine (Stockfish) runs entirely on your device. There is no analytics and no advertising, and we do not sell personal information. Diagnostics are sent only if you choose to copy and share them yourself.

Storage and security

Cloud data is protected by database access rules: your synced progress is readable only by you, a game only by its two players, and a call only by the two players in it. No method of storage or transmission is perfectly secure.

Signing out on a shared device

When you sign out, the app offers to remove your data from the device (on by default). Your synced progress stays in your account. If a different account signs in on a device that last belonged to you, the app clears your data from the device first, so it is never merged into theirs.

Deleting your account

In the app, open Account and tap Delete account. After Google confirms it is you, the app deletes your synced data, your Quick match and Play nearby entries, your friend requests and friendships, the tournaments you host and your entries in others, the call records of your games and any game invites nobody has accepted; it replaces your name and photo with "Deleted player" and removes your chat lines in your other games (the moves and results stay for your opponents); then it deletes your sign-in account, and clears the device. If you have Pro, cancel it first in Manage subscription. Stripe keeps billing records it must keep by law. See Delete account. You can also ask us at [PLACEHOLDER: contact email].

Depending on where you live you may have further rights (for example under the GDPR or the CCPA). Contact us to use them.
